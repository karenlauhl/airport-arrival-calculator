How that adds up

Where this comes from. The framework is Nate Silver's How soon should you arrive at the airport? — a 60-minute baseline inside the terminal, plus signed adjustments, and a commute estimate rounded up 30%.

What's been changed. Silver's numbers are calibrated for US airports: TSA PreCheck, US domestic flights, JFK/DFW/O'Hare as the busy tier. None of that transfers to Belfast or Dublin, so the adjustment categories here are local ones — paid Fast Track in place of PreCheck, Common Travel Area flights in place of "domestic", and Dublin's US preclearance, which has no US equivalent. The arithmetic is his; the calibration is an adaptation and should be treated as such.

This is a planning aid, not a guarantee. Silver missed about 0.5% of 800 flights using it — mostly by misjudging the commute, not the airport.
