    The .ics works with Apple Calendar, Outlook and most others: a "leave home" event with reminders 15 minutes before and on time, plus the flight itself.

    Where this comes from. The framework is Nate Silver's How soon should you arrive at the airport? — a 60-minute baseline inside the terminal, plus signed adjustments, and a commute estimate rounded up 30%.

    What's been changed. Silver's numbers are calibrated for US airports: TSA PreCheck, US domestic flights, JFK/DFW/O'Hare as the busy tier. None of that transfers to the UK or Ireland, so the adjustment categories here are local ones — paid Fast Track in place of PreCheck, Common Travel Area flights in place of "domestic", and Dublin's US preclearance, which has no US equivalent. Airport size is set by one rule from annual passenger numbers (tiny under 1m, small under 5m, medium under 15m, large under 40m, very large above), and island airports skip the security-queue penalty. The arithmetic is his; the calibration is an adaptation and should be treated as such.

    This is a planning aid, not a guarantee. Silver missed about 0.5% of 800 flights using it — mostly by misjudging the commute, not the airport.
